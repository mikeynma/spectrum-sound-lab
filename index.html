Music for the ZX Spectrum's beeper and AY chip.

Six voices at once from the 48K's one-bit speaker, with envelopes, slides, echo and a chorused bass. It is our own engine, in Z80, checked tick by tick. And Tim Follin's 1987 Your Sinclair tune, arranged for it, then both tunes again on the 128K's AY chip.

On the AY: both tunes on the 128K's sound chip

Six Voices and Star Tip 2026 again, this time on the 128K's AY chip: three real channels instead of six turns on one bit. The lead has a channel, the bass is a chorused buzzer on another, and the harmony, echo and drums share the third. It is cleaner than the beeper, and the music takes about 2% of the machine.

Star Tip 2026: Tim Follin's 1987 tune, on six voices

In 1987 Your Sinclair printed a three-voice tune by Tim Follin as a type-in, and said he was working on a six-channel routine with chorus bass, echo, portamento and ADSR. We could not find that it was ever released. This is his tune on our six-voice engine, with an echo, a chorused bass and drums.

Six Voices: six channels on one bit

A minute of music on the 48K's beeper, six voices at once: a lead with its echo, an arpeggio or pad, a bass and its chorus, and drums. It has ADSR envelopes, slides and vibrato, all from one bit, and the border shows each voice's turn on the speaker. On a 128K the snare also plays on the AY.

Playing them

Load the tape

They are ordinary tapes: load them at any speed. The music starts as soon as the screen appears.

Tapes last built: 20 November 2026.

  1. In an emulator (ZX Spin, Retro Virtual Machine, Fuse and others): open the .tap on a 48K or a 128K. On a 128K, pick Tape Loader; on a 48K, type LOAD "". These are ordinary tapes, so fast loading is fine.
  2. Turn the sound up. On a 48K all six voices come from the beeper; on a 128K the snare also plays on the AY. The AY versions need a 128K or +2.
  3. Any key stops the tune; another plays it again from the top.
  4. On a real Spectrum: play the tape into the EAR socket from a tape player or a TZX player.

About

How it's made

Our own tools

An assembler and a Spectrum emulator written for this work, in JavaScript. The emulator logs every change of the speaker and every AY write to the T-state, and turns them back into sound.

Checked, tick by tick

A model of the engine in JavaScript says what every voice should be doing after every tick. The build plays each tune on both machines and compares the Z80 with it the whole way through.

Then listened to

A check can pass and still sound wrong, so every tune is rendered to a recording, drawn as a spectrogram and played in other emulators.

Spectrum Sound Lab is a set of sound experiments for the ZX Spectrum, written in Z80 assembly with our own assembler and emulator, and checked tick by tick. Made in the UK, by the maker of games for the ZX Spectrum Next and of Borders & Loaders.
